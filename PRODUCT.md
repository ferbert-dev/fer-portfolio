## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js (React, TypeScript), deployed on Vercel. Chosen by the owner. Blog content and a contact form are part of the site.

## Users

Decision makers looking to hire outside help for AI automation or software engineering:

- **Small and mid-sized businesses**: owners and operations managers whose teams spend hours on manual, repetitive work (email triage, document and invoice handling, data entry, support) and who want it done cheaper and faster.
- **Startups and product teams**: founders and engineering leads who need a senior engineer to build AI features, agents or backend systems quickly.
- **Larger companies**: engineering or innovation managers hiring a contractor for AI automation or backend/platform work.

They arrive from a referral, LinkedIn, GitHub or a blog post and are deciding whether this person can be trusted with real systems.

## Product Purpose

The website of an AI automation agency built around the Ferbert name (working name: Ferbert; legal form and suffix open, e.g. Ferbert Inc.). It is presented as a young agency/startup with a team, speaking as "we", not as a one-person freelancer, so it can grow. Founder: Igor Ferbert. It wins client work in three services:

1. **AI process audit**: map how a company works and identify the processes that AI can run cheaper and faster than people.
2. **AI agents and automation**: build agents, RAG systems over company knowledge, voice agents and automated pipelines.
3. **Software development**: backend and full-stack systems.

Engagements: fixed-scope automation projects delivered end to end, or embedded engineering support for a client team. Success means qualified enquiries through the contact page.

## Positioning

A backend engineer with production experience (Java, Kotlin, Spring Boot, Kafka, Google Cloud, Kubernetes, Terraform, observability) who now builds AI agents. The difference from AI-hype agencies: agents built with production engineering underneath (idempotency, retries, monitoring, human review), and proof in the form of AI systems actually running in production.

## Product Principles

1. Show, don't claim: lead with systems that actually run, not adjectives.
2. Speak in business outcomes (hours saved, errors avoided) first, technology second.
3. Engineering credibility is the differentiator; every AI claim should be backed by how it is built to be reliable.
4. One clear next step: start a conversation about what to automate.
